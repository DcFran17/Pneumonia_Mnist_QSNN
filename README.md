This work investigates the use of a Quantum Spiking Neural Network (QSNN) based on the Alpha neuron for pneumonia classification from chest X-ray images. The proposed model was evaluated on the PneumoniaMNIST dataset, using 28×28 grayscale images and a binary classification scheme: Normal and Pneumonia. The network consists of a 1000-neuron hidden layer and a 2-neuron output layer, operating over 25 temporal steps. The model achieved a reported test accuracy of 84.13%, with a weighted precision of 0.8446, weighted recall of 0.8413, and weighted F1-score of 0.8424. During training, the loss decreased from 16.81 to 15.48 over five epochs, indicating that the network learned discriminative representations from the input images.

The confusion matrix shows successful classification of both classes, with 171 Normal and 362 Pneumonia samples correctly classified. The Alpha neuron also exhibits exponential decay of its internal memory in the absence of input, illustrating the temporal dynamics underlying the spiking computation. These results demonstrate the potential of quantum spiking neural networks for chest X-ray classification and provide a basis for further evaluation using multiple random seeds and quantum-noise or hardware-oriented simulations.
